The buyer signals worth acting on are the few that show up before your past wins and suggest a real change in the buyer's situation. Pick three to five, tie each to one specific action, and ignore the rest. Tracking everything just buries the signals that matter.

A go-to-market consultant I spoke with recently made the point simply. You can't track everything. Somebody looked at one web page? That's not material.

Signal tools have made it easy to collect hundreds of data points about every account. They haven't made it easier to know which ones mean anything.

## The tension: data versus focus

On one hand, more data feels safer. You don't want to miss a buyer who's ready. On the other hand, a rep with fifty alerts a day trusts none of them. Eventually they stop looking.

I land on fewer, better signals. A short list your team believes in will beat a firehose they ignore.

## What makes a signal material

A material signal does two things.

### It changes what the buyer is likely to do

A single page view doesn't change much. A new revenue leader starting at a target account does. So does a funding round, a hiring push for the role your product supports, or a contract renewal date coming up. These are changes in the buyer's situation, not just their clicks.

### It showed up before your past wins

This is the real test. Look at deals you've won in the last year or two. What happened at those accounts in the month or two before the first meeting? If a signal shows up often before wins and rarely before dead ends, it's material for you.

## How to pick your signals

Here's a simple way to cut the list:

1. **List every signal you currently collect.** It'll be long.
2. **Check each against your recent wins.** Did it appear before them? How often?
3. **Check against losses and no-shows.** Does it fire just as often for accounts that went nowhere?
4. **Keep the three to five that separate wins from everything else.**

If you don't have enough deals to test properly, start with the signals that indicate a situation change and refine as you go.

## Give every signal one action

A signal without an action is just a notification. For each one you keep, define:

- **What happens.** A personal email, a call, an introduction request.
- **Who does it.** A named role, not "sales."
- **How fast.** Same day, same week.
- **What the message says.** A short, relevant reason for reaching out.

For example: a target account's new sales leader starts. Within a week, the account owner sends a short note offering one useful idea for their first ninety days. No pitch.

## Measure whether it works

After a quarter, check each signal. Did outreach on it produce meetings? Did those meetings turn into real opportunities? Keep the ones that do. Drop the ones that don't. Add new candidates one at a time.

This is where most teams skip a step. They set up alerts and never look at whether the alerts produced anything.

## Signals don't replace targeting

One more caution. Signals work best on a list of accounts that already fit your ideal customer profile. A strong signal from a company you can't help is still a waste of a rep's afternoon. Get the target right first, then use signals to decide when to reach out.

If you haven't written the target down, start by [defining an ICP you can actually sell to](/blog/define-icp-stop-selling-features.html).

## Signs you're drowning in signals

- Reps get dozens of alerts a day and act on almost none.
- Nobody can name which signals preceded your last five wins.
- Outreach triggered by "intent" gets the same reply rate as cold outreach.
- Your signal tool's dashboard is open more in demos than in daily work.

## What to do this week

Take your last ten closed-won deals. For each, look at the 60 days before the first meeting and write down what happened at that account: new leader, funding, hiring, a pricing page visit, nothing. Count which signals show up most. That short list is your first draft of material signals, built from your own wins rather than a vendor's defaults.

## Common mistakes

- **Buying a signal tool before defining the ICP.** Signals on the wrong accounts are still noise.
- **Treating every signal the same.** A new leader and a single page view don't deserve the same response.

You have already paid a fractional person. A plan showed up. The stages did not change, and you are still the one who prices the large jobs. Questions to ask a fractional CRO start from that fact, before anyone discusses a retainer.

Picture a 90-person metal fabricator. A fractional operator delivered a commercial plan and sat in on meetings. The estimator still built every quote. The pipeline still had stages nobody used the same way. Picture the same stall at a software company: the founder still prices the large deals, a deck got left behind, and the sellers still disagree on what a qualified deal is. Either mess is what the next conversation has to be about.

## What should you ask about the sales motion?

Skip the biography. Ask them to walk one deal for a company like yours, from the first conversation to a dated next step. A fabricator, a distributor, a contractor, a software company. If every story they tell comes from a different kind of business than yours, you are in the wrong conversation.

Ask what they would open in the CRM in the first week, and what they would leave alone. A useful answer names the stages, a dated next step, and what makes a job qualified. A weak answer names a workshop.

Ask what they do while you are still the best closer. The work is to get that onto a page the team can run. [What a fractional CRO does](/blog/what-does-a-fractional-cro-do.html) is the job, as I sell it. [The fractional CRO page](/fractional-cro/) is the scope.

## Who keeps the motion moving between visits?

Name the person on your payroll who changes a stage, coaches the seller, and stops a bad quote on a day the fractional CRO is with another client. If the name you get is whoever is free that day, the motion stops when the visit ends, and you become the system again.

I do not manage reps day to day. A full-time sales leader is a different purchase. The only fees I will put next to this conversation are my own, on [fractional CRO cost](/blog/fractional-cro-cost.html). I do not quote a rate for the category.

## How do you know who will actually show up?

Some searches introduce you to a firm, then assign you a person. Ask, in the first meeting, whether the human in front of you is the human who will sit in your forecast. If they cannot say yes, you are buying an introduction.

[A fractional CRO network alternative](/compare/fractional-cro-network-alternative/) is how I publish that line: one named person, not a bench. I run the work myself. There is nobody else to send.

## What changes in the questions after a bad engagement?

Write one sentence on what did not move. The first seller. Your calendar. The stage names. Ask the next candidate what they would do about that sentence, in a company of your shape. A general program with your industry pasted in is the last meeting, again.

If the company is still an owner and one seller, the closer title is a [fractional sales leader](/fractional-sales-leader/), the person who builds the process the first reps can run. A fractional CRO is a later title, for a team that already sells. The larger title, dropped onto the smaller problem, is how the last engagement got vague.

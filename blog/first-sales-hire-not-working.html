When the first sales hire is not working

Colin Dowling November 2026 6 min read
Key takeaways
  1. The reflex to add a seller shows up before anyone names the real constraint.
  2. If the team cannot say who you are for, another rep adds noise.
  3. Pitching features at almost any account is a missing ICP, not a lazy rep.
  4. Hire when the message converts and capacity is the limit.

The first sales hire is not working, in most of the cases I see, because the job was two jobs. Invent the way you sell, and produce a number with it. The knee-jerk next step is another salesperson. That is often a clarity problem, not a capacity problem.

If your best rep left tomorrow, could a new person say in one sentence who you are for and why a buyer should switch? If not, the next req adds noise.

What is usually missing?

Who the customer is. Teams pitch features to almost any account that will take a meeting. That is not a hustle problem. It is an ICP that was never written. Define an ICP you can sell to is the practical version.

What problem you solve, in the buyer's words. If the sellers cannot name it the same way, they will each invent one.

Proof. A claim with no customer, no number, and no case is a slogan. I will not invent a case study to fill the gap. If you do not have one, say so, and sell the diagnosis instead.

The page on the first sales hire is the same argument from the hiring side. How to get out of founder-led sales is the step before the req.

What should you do instead of posting the role?

Write the one sentence. Check it against the last ten wins and the last ten losses. If the wins do not cluster, you do not have a hiring problem yet.

A fractional sales leader is the role that builds that sentence and the process around it. A fractional CRO is a later title, for a team that already sells. I do not place either person. I build the motion or I don't.

Questions this post answers

Why is our first sales hire not working?

Usually because the hire was asked to invent the target, the story, and the process while also hitting a number. One person cannot discover the market and run it in the same quarter.

Should we hire another salesperson?

Ask first whether a new hire could explain who you are for and why a buyer should switch. If the current team cannot answer that the same way, another seller multiplies the confusion.

When does headcount actually help?

When the message already converts and the pipeline is past what the current people can work. Adding sellers to a thin, unfocused pipeline adds activity. It does not add a market.

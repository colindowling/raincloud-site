Why is our outbound not working?

Colin Dowling November 2026 6 min read
Key takeaways
  1. Volume multiplies whatever the list and the message already are.
  2. A market that ignores cold outreach will not improve because you hired more senders.
  3. Point the activity at a real trigger before you add steps.
  4. Measure replies from the right accounts, not emails sent.

Why is our outbound not working is usually the wrong diagnosis of a busy team. The sends are real. The aim is not. Volume multiplies a loose list and a vague reason to write. It does not repair them. I see it every quarter.

I see the same pattern in companies that already have a sales team. The quarter is soft, so the sequence gets another step. The market gets another generic note. Some of those people were going to be buyers. Now they remember you as noise.

Why does more activity make it worse?

Activity pointed the wrong way wastes the same way a budget pointed at a bad return does. You can count the touches. You cannot count the buyer who will not take the meeting next year.

A market that is already resistant to cold outreach will not flip because the team got bigger. Hiring more senders into that market produces activity, not pipeline. Outbound that is not landing is the longer version of that symptom.

What should you check before the next sequence?

Three questions, in this order.

Who is on the list, and why would they talk to you this month? If you cannot answer, do not send.

What happens after a reply? If nobody owns the next step, the meeting was theater.

What are you counting? Emails sent tell you the team was busy. Replies from accounts that fit tell you the motion works. The post on burning the market with outbound is the same argument from the brand side.

An agency will still send if you pay it to. That is a different purchase from owning the motion. Outbound agency vs fractional CRO is that distinction.

Questions this post answers

Why is our outbound not working if the team is busy?

Busy is not the same as aimed. If the list is loose or the note has no reason to exist, more sends produce more silence. Tighten who you talk to and why this month, then look at volume.

Should we hire more people to do outbound?

Not into a market that already ignores cold outreach, and not before the target is real. A larger team pointed the wrong way burns the list faster. Fix the aim first.

Is the copy the problem?

Sometimes. It is the third question, not the first. Who received it, and what they were supposed to do next, usually explain the quiet before the sentence does.

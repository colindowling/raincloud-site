Forecast accuracy starts with the stage

Colin Dowling November 2026 6 min read
Key takeaways
  1. Forecast accuracy is a stage-definition problem before it is a math problem.
  2. Coverage built on dead deals is not coverage.
  3. Every open opportunity needs a dated next step or it is not in the number.
  4. Diagnose the pipeline before you add a tool to report it.

Forecast accuracy is bad when the stages are private languages. Pipeline problem diagnosis starts there. If two reps can put the same deal in different stages and both feel honest, the number in the meeting is a guess with a logo on it.

You do not fix that by adding a column. You fix it by writing what must be true before a deal moves, and taking out the deals that have no next step.

Why do forecasts drift?

Stage definitions that mean different things to different people. The pipeline not growing page is that symptom in one place.

Deals with no dated next step, still sitting in the commit. Hope is not a stage.

Coverage math on opportunities that are already dead. The board sees a multiple. The quarter sees a miss.

A single-threaded deal that was never going to close, counted the same as one with the buying group in the room. Why deals die after the yes is the late version of the same leak.

What does a usable forecast require?

Written entry and exit criteria for every stage. The same words, used by every rep.

A rule for silence. No activity inside a defined window, and the deal is flagged or closed out.

A number the pipeline can actually produce, not a target pasted on top of it. I will not promise you a win rate. I will tell you whether the current stages can support the number you already told the board.

What should you do this week?

Pull ten open deals. Ask, for each one, what happened last, what happens next, and on what date. The ones with no date are not forecast. They are inventory.

Questions this post answers

Why is our forecast accuracy so poor?

Because the stages do not mean the same thing to every rep. One person's proposal is another person's first meeting. The rollup is then a sum of private definitions, and the room stops believing it.

What is pipeline problem diagnosis?

Finding the stage where deals actually die, and the reason, before you add leads or headcount. More pipeline into a leaking stage leaks faster.

Can a tool fix the forecast?

A tool will report whatever you type. If exit criteria are not written and enforced, the dashboard is a faster way to be wrong. The scorecard belongs in the CRM you already have.

How to sell with no compelling event

Colin Dowling November 2026 6 min read
Key takeaways
  1. A feature buyers already replace for free will not create urgency.
  2. The workaround is the competitor, and it has no line on the budget.
  3. Urgency comes from the buyer's own hours, delays, and risk.
  4. If the pain is truly cheap, qualify out instead of forcing the forecast.

How to create urgency when a prospect has no compelling event: stop leading with the feature they already worked around. Price the workaround they hate, in hours, delay, and risk, using their numbers. Then let them compare that figure with yours.

Assume the team has already found a way to live with the problem. They made it this far without you. The status quo is often the next best alternative, and it does not have a logo.

Why does the feature pitch die?

You lead with the headache your product was built to remove. Nobody moves. Not because the headache is fake. Because they already routed around it. The route is ugly. It is also familiar, and it does not ask for budget.

If you cannot say what they do today, you are pitching into a blank. They are not in a blank. They are in a workaround.

How do you price a workaround?

Ask how it works. Who touches it. What breaks when that person is out.

Ask what it costs. Hours. Slipped handoffs. The deal that waits because the spreadsheet is the system. People risk, when one person is the process.

Then compare, out loud, with your price. Their arithmetic, not yours. "This might be nicer" is not a date. "This consumes a person's time every month" can become one.

Some deals already have a compelling event: a renewal, an audit, a new leader, a target the current setup cannot carry. Ask about the next six months and listen for dates. If there is no date and the workaround is genuinely cheap, qualify out. Forcing it fills the forecast with hope. Forecast accuracy dies that way. Complaints are not demand is the same test from the pain side.

What if a lot of deals sit at "interested"?

Questions this post answers

What is a compelling event in sales?

A date or a circumstance that forces a decision. A renewal, a deadline, a new leader's plan, a system that failed. Without one, doing nothing has no visible cost, and the deal drifts.

How do you sell against a homegrown workaround?

Do not attack it. Ask how it works, who maintains it, and what it costs in hours, delay, and risk. Compare that with your price using their numbers. The workaround feels free because nobody tracks it.

Why do interested buyers never purchase?

The pain is real and tolerable. Interest is not demand. The test is whether they will pay to make it stop, which depends on whether anyone has priced the cost of leaving it alone.

The Problem

Five symptoms. One underlying problem.

These are the things I hear on fit calls. They are also sections 01 through 10 of the audit, which is the point: they are symptoms of a motion that was never architected, and fixing them in the wrong order is why the last attempt didn’t hold.

01

Outbound that burns out

You spray sequences and hope. No signal-based triggers. No ICP definition tight enough to build a system around. Reps burn leads faster than you can source them.

What the audit looks at →
02

Content that doesn’t convert

You’re publishing. Nobody is buying. The content isn’t mapped to the buyer journey. There’s no bottom-of-funnel anchor. Traffic doesn’t know what to do next.

What the audit looks at →
03

Channels that don’t talk to each other

Outbound, content, and your CRM all running in parallel, not in concert. There’s no Engine: no architecture where each channel makes the others work harder.

What the audit looks at →
04

Deals falling through the cracks

The follow-up nobody sent. The stalled deal nobody flagged. The next step that never got booked. Your process depends on every rep remembering everything, every time, and they don’t.

What the audit looks at →
05

A forecast nobody believes

Stage definitions that mean different things to different reps. Coverage math built on deals that are already dead. You are forecasting against a number that cannot happen, and everyone in the room knows it.

The Method

Diagnose it. Rebuild it. Hand it over.

01

Diagnose.

One hundred points across ten sections of your go-to-market, scored against your CRM data and four to six interviews with your team. You get a number, a signed baseline, and every finding — ranked by what it costs you in pipeline, with the cost of each quantified.

02

Rebuild.

Ninety days. ICP rebuilt from closed-won data, positioning and message map, the pipeline model, the outbound build, the scorecard live in your CRM, the playbook. Same senior person every month. There is no team behind me and no one to hand you off to.

03

Hand over.

From month four your team runs it and I measure it. The fee falls to $7,500 and my job becomes finding what’s leaking, naming who owns it and by when, and telling you the truth about whether it’s working.

Most GTM builds fail in the quarter after the consultant leaves. The handover is not the end of this engagement — it’s the half that’s priced to last.

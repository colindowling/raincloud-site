The Standing Hour

One hour a week, same slot, with someone who has already built what you’re building.

$4,500 a month. Six clients at a time. You do the work; I make sure it’s the right work, in the right order, and I’m there every week to check.

The hour

It runs like a music lesson, which is the most useful way I’ve found to describe it. You practise between lessons. The lesson is where the work gets corrected and extended. Nobody expects the teacher to go home and practise for them.

15 minutes

Review

What you committed to last week, what actually happened, what the numbers did.

10 minutes

Recalibrate

What that result changes. Usually the interesting question is whether the plan was wrong rather than the execution.

15 minutes

Plan

This week’s commitments, specific enough that we can both tell next Tuesday whether they happened.

20 minutes

Teach

One piece of the motion, picked by your score. If something is on fire, the fire gets the twenty minutes and we pick up next week.

What we work through

Your score decides the order, and the weakest sections get the most weeks. This is the map, not a syllabus:

  • Who you actually sell to, and who you’ve been selling to
  • The problem in your buyer’s words rather than yours
  • Positioning against the real alternatives, including doing nothing
  • Where buyers compare you, and what they find
  • Your bottom-of-funnel gaps
  • Building a list that’s a market rather than a download
  • Signals worth acting on, and the ones everyone wastes time on
  • The first touch
  • Follow-up that isn’t nagging
  • Speed to lead, routing, and the leaks between marketing and sales
  • Discovery that qualifies instead of interrogating
  • Forecast hygiene, and what your numbers are actually telling you

It doesn’t complete. Every quarter the score gets re-run and the order changes, because your market does.

Office hours

Two 2-hour blocks a week, split into 30-minute slots. Book one a week whenever you see an opening — a deal you want a second opinion on before a call, a sequence you want read before it goes out, a hiring decision.

They don’t bank and they don’t roll over. There are more slots than clients on purpose: office hours that are always full aren’t office hours, they’re a second session nobody priced.

What you own

Everything. Every list, every agent, every sequence, every document, every framework. All of it gets built in the session, on your screen, in your accounts.

If we stop working together, nothing breaks and nothing has to be handed over, because there was never anything on my side to hand.

What I don’t do

  • I don’t run your outbound, write your content, or manage your campaigns.
  • I don’t build or maintain anything between sessions.
  • I don’t manage your reps.
  • I don’t book meetings or carry a number on your behalf.

And I don’t guarantee a revenue outcome, because the work is yours and so is the result. If you want someone to be accountable for your pipeline instead of you, I’m the wrong call — and I’d rather say that here than three months in.

The Milestone Analysis

At the end of your first term we stop and look properly. Your score re-run, side by side with the first one. What you committed to each week against what you actually did. And then a straight recommendation from me:

  • Keep building at $4,500, because there’s real work left and you’re doing it.
  • Move to maintenance at $2,500 every other week, because the motion is built and running and what you need now is a governor rather than a teacher.
  • Or we’re done — and if that’s what the numbers say, I’ll say it. An engagement where the work doesn’t get done isn’t worth either of our money, and I’d rather give the seat to someone who’ll use it.

Questions

How is this different from a fractional CRO?

A fractional CRO runs your revenue function. I don’t run anything. You keep the job; I make sure you’re doing it well.

Can you just do it for me?

No. I built that version of this and I didn’t like what it turned me into. There are good people who do it — I’m happy to point you at one.

What if I miss a week?

Eleven sessions a quarter is built for that. Miss more than two and we should talk about whether the timing is right.

Do I need a CRM?

Not to start. You’ll need something before long, and setting it up properly is often one of the first things we do together.

Can my team join?

Yes, and often they should. One of them will end up owning part of this, and they’ll own it better if they were in the room.

What if it isn’t working?

That’s what the Milestone Analysis is for. But you’ll know before then, and so will I — the review block makes it very hard to hide a quarter where nothing moved.

Why only six clients?

Because I do this myself, around a full-time role, and seven would make all seven worse. When it’s full it’s full, and if I tell you there’s a wait, there’s a wait.

Is this B2B only?

Yes. Everything I know is B2B, most of it with considered purchases and a real sales conversation. If you sell to consumers I’d be guessing, and you’d be paying for it.

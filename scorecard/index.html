The Scorecard

100 points. Yours will fail about half of them.

That’s normal. Which half is the part that matters.

The Scorecard is a structured read of your entire go-to-market — how you’re found, how you’re compared, how you reach out, how you follow up, and what your numbers are actually telling you. Every point is marked pass, partial or fail, and the findings are ranked by what each one is costing you rather than by how easy it is to fix.

Why you don’t score it yourself

You can have the hundred points. I’d rather you had them.

But scoring your own go-to-market is like proofreading your own writing. You know what you meant, so you read what you intended rather than what’s there. Half the value is in having someone who has built this four or five times look at it cold and tell you which of the failures actually costs you money.

What happens after

Nothing, if you want. The Scorecard stands on its own and plenty of people take it and go do the work themselves. That’s a good outcome.

If you’d rather not do it alone, the score becomes the plan for the Standing Hour — and starting with the Scorecard shortens the minimum commitment from six months to three, because I already know what I’m walking into.

Fractional CRO vs VP of sales: which job are you hiring?

A VP sits on your payroll and runs the team. A fractional CRO runs your revenue function part of the time. I’m neither. I don’t run anything. You keep the job; I make sure you’re doing it well.

A standing hour every week with someone who has held the VP Sales seat three times and built the motion you’re building now. I score your go-to-market against 100 dimensions, we work the gaps in the order that matters, and everything we build is yours.

When is a VP the right hire?

Hire a VP when the motion already converts and the team is out of capacity. Adding a VP to a broken motion gives you a salary and the same forecast.

If you have a sales team that needs managing rather than a motion that needs designing, that’s a different problem and a different person. A VP is the right call.

When is a VP the wrong first move?

When nobody can say who you sell to, what a qualified deal is, or why you win. A new VP inherits that fog and is judged on a number the motion cannot produce. The diagnostic is the 100-Point Scorecard. The free look before it is the 20 Minute Teardown.

If the company is still founder-led, the closer comparison is a fractional sales leader, not a chief.

How it works

  • 1. The 20 Minute Teardown. Free. Forty of the hundred dimensions, scored from public sources only, nothing needed from you. A recorded walkthrough and the scorecard, ending with two or three things to go and do right now for immediate impact.
  • 2. The 100-Point Scorecard. $5,000. The full score, including the sixty dimensions that need a look inside. Five business days. Buy it on its own, or let it take month one of the engagement at no extra cost.
  • 3. The Standing Hour. $4,500 a month. Eleven sessions a quarter, same slot every week. Office hours twice a week in 30-minute blocks. Three-month minimum if you started with the Scorecard, six if you didn’t.
  • 4. The Milestone Analysis. At the end of your first term: your score re-run, what you committed to against what you actually did, and a straight recommendation. Keep building at $4,500, or move to maintenance at $2,500 every other week. Sometimes the recommendation is that I’ve taken you as far as I can, and I’ll tell you that too.

What you own

Everything. Every list, every agent, every sequence, every document, every framework. All of it gets built in the session, on your screen, in your accounts.

If we stop working together, nothing breaks and nothing has to be handed over, because there was never anything on my side to hand.

What I don’t do

  • I don’t run your outbound, write your content, or manage your campaigns.
  • I don’t build or maintain anything between sessions.
  • I don’t manage your reps.
  • I don’t book meetings or carry a number on your behalf.

And I don’t guarantee a revenue outcome, because the work is yours and so is the result. If you want someone to be accountable for your pipeline instead of you, I’m the wrong call — and I’d rather say that here than three months in.

Questions people ask before they hire.

Should I hire a VP of sales or a fractional CRO?

Hire a VP when the motion already converts and the team is out of capacity. A fractional CRO runs your revenue function. If you intend to run it yourself, the alternative is the Standing Hour: you keep the job; I make sure you’re doing it well.

Do you replace a VP?

No. I don’t manage your reps, and I don’t book meetings or carry a number on your behalf. If you still need a VP, you hire one into a system that exists.

What does each one cost?

I do not publish a VP salary, because I do not hire them and I will not invent a number. The 20 Minute Teardown is free. The 100-Point Scorecard is $5,000 standalone, or it takes month one at no extra cost. The Standing Hour is $4,500 a month.

What if we are earlier than a VP?

If the founder is still the system, read the founder-led sales trap and the first sales hire page. A title will not substitute for a written process.

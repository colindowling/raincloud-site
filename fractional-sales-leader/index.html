Fractional sales leader, fractional sales leadership, and a fractional CSO.

What is a sales process for a 50 person company?

A 50 person company rarely needs another chief. It needs a process a new seller can follow without the founder in the room.

  • Who you sell to, written tightly enough that a rep can disqualify an account.
  • What a qualified opportunity is, and the next step every open deal must have.
  • The story the founder tells, written down so the next person can tell it. Why founder-led sales stalls.
  • A first hire who is not asked to invent the system and hit a number in the same quarter. When the first sales hire fails.

What fractional sales leadership is not

It is not a network that assigns you whoever is free. It is not a recruiter. It is not someone who carries your quota. I run two builds at a time, and I do not manage reps day to day. Fees and the six-month minimum are on the pricing page.

Questions people ask before they hire.

What is a fractional sales leader?

A fractional sales leader builds the sales process, the message, and the first operating rhythm, then hands them to the people who will run them. It is not a full-time VP and it is not an open-ended coaching retainer.

How is a fractional sales leader different from a fractional CRO?

A fractional CRO is the revenue seat: forecast, pricing, the whole motion. A fractional sales leader is the selling system the first reps use. At $50 million to $200 million I still start with the autopsy, because the title is usually ahead of the process.

What is a sales process for a 50 person company?

A 50 person company usually needs a written way to find, qualify, and close, not a second executive. Who you sell to, what a good deal looks like, and the next step every open deal must have. That is the work. The title can wait.

Do you recruit the leader?

No. I do not place fractional executives and I do not run a bench. If the motion needs a rebuild, that is the 90-day engagement after the autopsy. If you need a hire, that is your search.

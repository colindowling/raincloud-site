The premise

The engagement is supposed to need you less every month

Most GTM retainers are flat. You pay the same fee in month twelve that you paid in month one, and the incentive is to keep you there at that number — not to build something that runs without the person you’re paying.

This one is built to hand over.

Month one is the heaviest work and the longest-compounding: the diagnosis, the ICP, the positioning, the pipeline model. By month four it is built, your team is running it, and my job changes from building the motion to measuring it and telling you the truth about it.

So the fee steps down $2,500 a month, and the largest step is the one into month four — because that is the month the work actually changes hands. It starts at twice the ongoing rate and it lands on it.

You are not buying hours. You are buying a motion that works and a transfer of it to the people who have to run it after I’m gone.

FAQ

The questions people actually ask

Why does the price go down instead of up?

Because the work changes. Month one is diagnosis and rebuild — the heaviest lift and the longest-compounding. By month four your team is running the motion and I’m measuring it. The fee follows the work, which is how it should have always been.

Why is the audit required?

Because I won’t sell a six-month engagement off a sales call and you shouldn’t buy one. Ten days and $5,000 is how we both find out whether this is worth doing.

Why isn’t the audit credited toward the build?

Because then it isn’t an audit, it’s a deposit. The audit is a real piece of work with its own deliverable, and about half of them don’t go further.

What if we do the audit and decide not to continue?

You keep the score, the baseline, the five findings and the business case. Plenty of companies take that and run the fix themselves. If you want someone keeping you honest while you do, that’s the $4,000 Scorecard.

Is there a guarantee?

No money-back guarantee. I don’t control whether your team executes, so I won’t price as though I do. The audit is the risk reversal: a small, bounded first step before any commitment.

Can we skip the audit if we already know what’s wrong?

No. You may well be right about what’s wrong — most people are right about one of the five and miss the other four.

Who actually does the work?

I do. There’s no team behind me and no one to hand you off to. That’s also why I run two builds at a time.

What do you need from us?

One named executive sponsor, read-only CRM access from day one, four to six people for interviews during the audit, and a team willing to execute after day 90.

What happens if our team doesn’t execute?

You’ll know by the second monthly review, because the scorecard will show it, and I’ll say so. That’s most of what you’re paying for from month four.

Can we pause?

Not during the six-month minimum. After that, Advisory is month to month and you can pause, stop, or step down to the Scorecard.

What’s the difference between the two ongoing tiers?

Advisory includes a monthly work session and weekly office hours — you get working time. The Scorecard is measurement and direction only: the monthly review, the mid-month check, the quarterly re-score.

Do you work with companies outside $50M–$200M?

Sometimes, below the range. Above it there’s usually an internal team and the work is different. Ask on the fit call.

FAQ

The obvious questions, answered plainly.

What is an AI agent, in plain terms?

A worker that does one job, repeatedly, inside the tools you already use. It watches for a trigger (a call ends, a deal goes quiet, a lead comes in), does the work (drafts the recap, flags the risk, routes the lead), and writes the result back to your CRM. A person stays in the loop wherever judgment is needed.

Do I have to replace my CRM or change my stack?

No. The agents connect in. Your CRM stays your system of record. If your tool has an API, MCP server, or webhooks, I can build in it.

Who owns it after it is built, and who fixes it when it breaks?

The agents run in your accounts, so you own them. I operate them: weekly tuning against live data, monitoring, and incident response. Someone named owns it when it breaks, and it is not your team's job to notice.

Why not just build one myself with Claude or a no-code tool?

You can, and for a single agent that is often fine. The problem shows up at agent number five: nobody can say what is running, what it touches, who owns it, or why it stopped working in March. The value is in the operating layer, not the first build.

Is this only for sales and marketing?

This page covers the sales and marketing workers, which is where I start. Multi-function coverage (finance, operations, governance) is available on request.

How does this relate to the GTM Audit?

The GTM Audit is the front door. With the agent opportunity map as an add-on, it shows which workers close the widest cracks first, and the $7,500 credits in full toward the engagement.
